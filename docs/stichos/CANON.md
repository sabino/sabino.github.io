# Destino: Stíchos — adaptation notes

The user supplied the story “Destino: Stíchos” in full on 10 September 2026 and clarified the planet and traveler on 11 September 2026. These notes preserve the facts that control this game adaptation. They are not a rewrite or continuation presented as the author's original text.

**Stíchos is the planet.** Its name remains accented in the title and planetary labels. **Vespera** is the starting city, and its main church is the **Cathedral of Vespera**. Vespera is an original adaptation name, not a city name supplied in the source story.

Theo Bishop comes from the future. He is exceptionally intelligent and has completed many earlier missions across different planets and eras. That experience informs his observations and caution, but does not make him omniscient. He does not know the Sallas family's secret or why his signal failed. Any connection between the family and the interrupted transmission remains a possibility to investigate.

Mind transmission placed Theo in a priest's body by mistake. He has been stranded for ten Earth years, equivalent to twenty Stíchoi years, without contact with his base. The present year is 3886. One local year lasts six Earth months. His present identity on Stíchos is Theo Bishop; the body he currently inhabits has its own life and possessions.

The planet is extremely cold, with blue landscapes extending into the horizon. The inhabitants use the Rømer temperature scale. Cequin resembles rosemary and is necessary to breathe in the cold. Highly developed botany supplies food and medicine directly from plants. Industrial production threatens this society's established botanical practices.

Six great families are directed by priests, often their oldest members. The priests are revered as “originais,” true children of Prometheus. Their hidden Cúpula do Destino directs each family's destiny. Theo must publicly endorse decisions he does not support, and has avoided influencing history while trying to maintain his cover.

Priest Orlando of the Brown clan publicly seeks support for industrial food and medicine. Other clans perceive a threat. Theatre and surrealist paintings carry competing political messages before open war. Theo resolves to intervene cautiously to prevent catastrophe, despite his previous restraint. Theo suspects the Sallas secret could matter to that intervention and possibly to his return. Its content and its connection to his failed signal remain unknown to him.

The priest's cathedral is Gothic with a futuristic character: colored stained glass, monumental architecture, and radio-era technology. The author's reference matrix combines world-war tension, industrial-revolution economics, clan society, radio communication, Gothic cathedrals, surrealism, Prometheus, dictatorship, Neoplatonism, botany, and an ice age.

## Current playable interpretation

The opening shows a fragmented memory of the failed transmission, then returns to Theo's present in 3886. Body transfer is a mental event expressed through interrupted voices, double vision, breath, pulse, and remembered words. There is no physical rift or extraction gate in this mode.

The continuous world contains humanoid inhabitants and cold botanical settlements. Cequin, aid, trade, equipment, plant preparation, faction trust, and damaged radio infrastructure provide immediate actions. The Sallas investigation ties those actions to Theo's reason for being here. Brown and Sallas are source canon; the other generated clan names, NPC names, specific local tasks, and revealed clues are new adaptation material.

The game's present-day waking scene is not the first arrival on Stíchos. Theo has already spent twenty local years in the priest's body. Regaining a signal is progress toward understanding the transmission, not a claim that the entire Sallas mystery has been solved.

World layout, settlement details, local people, resources, and encounters vary with the seed. The author's narrative anchors remain coherent across seeds. The user explicitly prioritized humanoids for this iteration; the previous arbitrary-species experiment remains a separate reference implementation.

Physical belongings stay with each inhabited body. Theo’s physical notebook belongs to the original priest and remains with that body during a transfer. Another host can recall its contents but cannot open a paper book they do not carry; returning to the priest restores the actual notebook. The unique volume is separate from stackable supplies and satchel capacity. The traveler's experience, memories, promises, and discoveries survive a transfer. The first time a generated resident is inhabited, their role and seed supply finite possessions; returning to them restores their actual remaining belongings. Clinic recovery treats the same body and is shown differently from entering another person.
