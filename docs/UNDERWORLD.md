# The underground-to-town slice

This is original Verso code and Canvas2D artwork. Its public design reference is Cygnus Cross’s **Emberville**, specifically the official description of exploration, weapon-bound combos, survivor rescue and rebuilding. We did not play or reverse-engineer that unreleased game, reproduce its Vitromotus layout, use its characters/names, or import any assets. References: [Cygnus Cross](https://www.cygnuscross.com/), [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2295170/Emberville/?l=english). The borrowed principle is a connected underground/surface loop; all implementation, names, themes, rooms, creatures and effects here are original.

## Working slice

Every admitted settlement can have a version-1 complex with three connected floors: Drowned Masonry, Mycelial Works and Resonance Furnace. Each 58 × 44 floor uses an eight-room ring grammar, with seeded room dimensions, complex name and control order. A reachable archive inscription teaches the floor’s three-signal gate sequence. Its forward sluice and a one-sided return shortcut prevent bypassing that puzzle. A side branch contains a refuge, repair salvage and an actual hidden archive pocket. An open ascent always allows retreat; descending requires defeating that floor’s keeper. The final engine yields knowledge, crystal and a civilization relic, with a deep-forge surface unlock.

Eight place identities have actual uses: threshold/retreat; wet cistern with a timed conduit; archive with puzzle and knowledge; gauntlet with a warning vent and overseer; keeper vault/descent; foundry salvage; safe refuge/hearth; root gallery with pursuing scavengers. Surface materials follow location: stone, shallow water, dirt and metal. The core supplies biome and dungeon depth for the adaptive score and Foley consumer.

Six persistent enemies occupy each floor: close scavengers, ranged sentries, a corridor overseer and a keeper. Ranged sentries lock their bearing and telegraph for 0.8 seconds before a traveling projectile. Overseers use a line strike. Keepers begin with a fixed line strike, then change below half health to a radial pressure attack with at least 1.15 seconds’ anticipation and 1.6 seconds’ recovery. An interrupted ordinary foe recovers briefly; a keeper cannot be stun-locked by ordinary melee. Pressure traps have deterministic idle, warning and active phases, with a per-person hit cadence.

Attacks use the existing procedural `weaponProfile` from **authority-resolved** weapon kind and seed. Consecutive attacks in the timing window build to a third-hit finisher; melee gains a wider arc, ranged gains damage, and a staff finisher releases a three-projectile spread. Sword, bow and staff actions retain distinct speeds/ranges and cannot borrow another family’s ranged behavior. Guard reduces damage; a short evade window complements actual movement handled by the session. These are authority actions, not rendering outcomes.

Rescue the overseer’s captive to return a stable person to the settlement: a mason for waterworks, a botanist for a healing garden, and a machinist for a deep forge. The caller must consume these verified rescue/unlock events into the persistent property/worker system in the same room transaction. Survivors cannot be rescued repeatedly. Cleared enemies and their damage contributors remain in saves; rendering culling does not erase them. Active enemies use reusable bounded reverse path fields to pursue around corridor corners and through opened passages instead of resetting at room boundaries.

## Integration contract

The integrator owns session, protocol, authority, health, inventory, field economy, physical movement and room snapshots. This module does not authorize client balance or health claims.

- `new Underworld(worldSeed, optionalSave)` validates version 1 and seed. Corrupt state is rejected; there is no silent reset.
- `floor(settlementId, depth)` generates/adopts a deterministic plan. `frame(spaceId)` returns a plan, state, projectiles and time. Do **not** resend the plan’s 2,552 cells on each multiplayer frame: clients generate it from seed/settlement/depth and receive bounded state/projectile changes.
- `enter(peer, settlementId, trustedSurfaceEntrance)` requires a live authenticated traveler within 2.2 world tiles. Root resolves the actual entrance from generated surface geometry. It returns an explicit transition and stores the exact surface return address.
- `location(actorId)` is the authoritative space address. `syncPeer(peer)` accepts the established game’s accepted position in the **same** authorized space and an open rounded tile. It cannot authorize a floor change. This inherits the existing room’s movement trust model; it is not a claim of new full anti-speed-hack movement authority.
- `blocked(spaceId, x, y)` and `material(...)` adapt normal navigation/collision/Foley. Tile centers are integers, matching `InfiniteWorld`, not CSS pixels. Players move normally; only verified nearby stairs or entrance actions return transitions.
- `interact(peer, targetId, nowSeconds, sequence)` and `attack(peer, { kind, heading, sequence }, nowSeconds)` use monotonically increasing per-person action sequences retained in saves. Root supplies server time, membership and trusted equipment, never damage from a request. Root owns the namespace shared with its command coordinator; choose a sequence strictly above the saved resident sequence.
- `tick(dt, authenticatedAcceptedPeers, nowSeconds)` advances at most 0.25 seconds in ≤ 1/30-second slices. Apply returned hit events to actual health; route actual death events to `FieldEconomy.spawnDeath`. A floor without present actors retains its exact persistent state with no offline combat catch-up.
- Cache/core rewards become durable `pendingRewards(actorId)` entries. Root calls the exact `FieldEconomy.transact` with that stable reward token. **Only after success**, call `acknowledgeReward(actorId, rewardId)`. A full satchel must retain the pending entry and a visible retry action; it cannot erase the reward. Root must never expose arbitrary client acknowledgment commands.
- `leave(actorId)` removes owned projectiles at disconnect but retains resident identity, location and bounded attack cooldown, so a reconnect is not a free extra strike. A reconnect restores the authoritative underground address.
- `save()` is stored in `privateState.systems`, not the legacy signed public checkpoint. It contains topology addresses, room discoveries, clears, enemy identity/health/location, rescue marks, keys/shortcuts, pending rewards, contributors, surface return addresses and action sequence. It excludes projectiles, telegraphs, audio, VFX, pathfinding buffers and active timers. Restoring cancels interrupted attacks; it does not simulate offline damage. Strict validation rejects unknown keys, invalid bodies, duplicate identities, unsafe addresses and excess capacities.
- `drawUnderworld(ctx, frame, { width, height, x, y, tileSize, time, reducedMotion, intensity })` is a pure, view-culled original pixel-art pass. The main renderer overlays Verso’s existing player/peer sprites and retained HUD. Essential telegraphs and trap states stay visible when decorative motion is disabled. Root provides accessible close-reading descriptions and underground minimap correspondence instead of relying on glyphs alone.

Limits: 16 complexes × 3 floors × 6 enemies; 128 persistent travelers; 64 total projectiles; 256 events per tick; 256 pending salvage rewards. Admission stops instead of evicting owned history. Typed path fields are bounded by admitted floors and reused across updates. One room’s private snapshot must include economy and dungeon state atomically before advertising durable completion.

## Evidence and remaining integration checks

`node --experimental-strip-types --test tests/underworld.test.ts` covers 120 deterministic floor/seed/depth combinations, puzzle reachability and gate isolation, genuine stair proximity, cross-space rejection, permanent shortcuts, same-sequence replay, durable reward acknowledgment, rescued-trade uniqueness, procedural weapon combos, reconnect cooldown, warning-before-projectile, boss phase/recovery, trap cadence, save compatibility, malformed snapshot rejection and an actor pursuing around corridor corners into another room.

The raw authority microbenchmark is `.dream-loop/living-systems/underworld/probe.json`: 3 floors, 18 enemies, 8 actors, 1,500 simulation steps at 1/30 second and 500 copied frames. On this Linux/Node run, generation p95 was 0.212 ms, simulation p95 0.279 ms / maximum 2.748 ms, and full frame copy p95 0.263 ms. These are **pure Node core measurements**, not phone frame-rate evidence. Native integrated portrait/render, actual economy claim, rescued-worker arrival, three-floor combat, mobile controls and multiplayer room isolation remain the lead’s browser/integration gates.

Concrete later depth: alternate topology grammars, additional return loops, more authored encounter templates and hazards, destructible prop combat, richer puzzle types, multiple bosses per civilization and cross-complex migration. This production slice deliberately has three designed floor identities and one complete rescue/rebuilding chain per floor; it does not claim arbitrary dungeon depth or parity with unreleased reference-game content.

## Coordinator integration delivered in this batch

`LivingSystems` now owns the underground core and compact presentation. The authenticated command family is `underworld-enter` (settlement ID), `underworld-interact` (feature ID), `underworld-attack` (kind and heading), and `underworld-rewards`. It resolves an entrance from a real generated settlement notice, shrine, grave or doorway with an open exterior neighbor. Neither a room code nor a submitted coordinate authorizes a transition. Entrances and plot surveys are deterministically anchored to the settlement. Parcel checks are processed one candidate per discovery interval to avoid generating all approaches at once.

A command event ID ends in a positive safe integer (existing client `rN` request IDs are supported). The coordinator stores the highest accepted sequence for each authenticated life, allowing gaps from other protocol requests. `frame.nextSequence` and `nextSequence(actorId)` support resumed solo counters. Replaying an old request after the server’s short response cache expires still cannot attack or consume supplies again. `SystemsPeer.active` means a connected/playing life; `combatActive` is separate, so menus can craft or use a cache while pausing attacks and hostile targeting. Equipment is the authority’s frozen/generated kit in `SystemsPeer.weaponKind/weaponSeed` and `frame.equipment`, never a request-supplied damage value.

`frame.underground` carries floor identity/name/biome/material/dimensions, state, projectiles, time, discovered/nearby feature descriptions and a current objective. It does not transmit plan cells. `frame.location` is authoritative; after restart or reconnect it restores the resident’s saved floor address rather than accepting the client’s stale surface pose. Renderer/navigation integration uses `floorBlocked(spaceId,x,y)` plus the independently generated plan. `SystemsResult.transition` is explicit and confirmed. The entry response is canonically projected and never copies transient underworld event arrays into a request receipt.

All player-directed dungeon or guard hits go through **one** `drainCombatEvents()` stream. Command results do not duplicate that damage stream. The server bridges it into its existing numeric combat receipt/acknowledgment stream; solo consumes it once locally. Enemy and persistent-person health changes happen inside their respective authority before presentation. Fatal underworld damage reserves field-ledger capacity before committing; a bounded durable fallback queue retains any failed death-to-drop delivery. Successful cache credits require an acknowledged field transaction, and a full satchel retains its pending salvage.

Recovered plans also reduce the relevant construction coin fee by 25% in that settlement: waterworks supports sawmills/gardens, the healing garden supports apothecaries, and the deep forge supports carpentry. This relief never discounts deposits or generates currency. A rescue now admits a real stable surface actor into `ActorLedger`, with a generated original appearance and a mason/botanist/machinist profession. The person is employable through `PropertyWorld`; a nearby rescued engineer offers a real workshop crafting station, while a botanist can prepare hearth recipes. Rescue/unlock records persist and cannot spawn duplicates. If actor admission is saturated, the rescue remains undelivered and retries instead of falsely reporting that a worker arrived.

Field `ration`, `bandage` and `ward-kit` commands debit the authoritative satchel exactly once. A ration restores 8% maximum health and 45% stamina; a dressing restores 25% maximum health; a ward lasts thirty seconds and reduces coordinator combat damage by 30%. The caller applies returned restoration to actual health/stamina. `mitigateDamage` is available to other authoritative combat bridges; do not apply it twice to already mitigated coordinator hits.

The coordinator also canonicalizes civic actions before persistence. `CivicWorld.recordAction` independently copies only approved action fields, so session names, weapon metadata or future presentation extras cannot break its strict save schema. Persistent person attacks resolve the actual body and trusted weapon, enforce reach/facing/line of sight/cooldown, record credible assault or murder, update body health, and produce actual field drops on death. Guards act on their own witnessed/reported charge, allow six seconds to surrender at arrest range, and then use the single damage stream. A fine ends that confrontation while leaving the memory. Receiver-specific hostile presentation does not mark the guard universally hostile to unrelated players.

Existing homes are discovered by a bounded whole-building flood fill and a stable canonical doorway, with the real **outward** open neighbor. This fixes north-facing inward destinations and different prices/names depending on the approaching door. A verified solo origin home is adopted only at the actual nearby entrance; shared authority ignores unverified personal-save claims. Existing balances are never migrated into the trusted satchel. Clear outdoor plots expose reachable construction; roads, resources and structures are not erased. Rank-one craft guild membership grants after-hours workshop access; rank two discounts eligible construction/hiring/furnishing coin fees, without discounting deposits, withdrawals, leases or commodity transfers. Fee rounding remains integer and never creates a balance.

Trusted occupied-body bindings are optional saved `occupied` pairs. A life binds one body once; two lives cannot claim the same binding. Its original NPC identity/location remain in the actor ledger, updated from accepted player poses, while autonomous routines, coarse travel, hiring, gossip and contact selection are suspended. All receiver NPC frames omit bound bodies because the peer renderer owns them. Disconnect retains the binding and location. Other NPCs remain autonomous, and private guard hostility is calculated for each receiving player.

New coordinator save fields are optional when reading earlier local-batch snapshots: underground state, command floors, pending deaths, rescue/unlock records, wards, numeric-domain serial and occupied bindings. All are strict bounded domain records. Generated tile plans, path queues, transient event arrays, PTT, microphone samples and renderer objects are excluded.

`tests/living-systems-integration.test.ts` contains nineteen connected integration regressions, covering canonical civic persistence, north/south home equivalence and verified solo adoption, menus/replay, authentic entry and saved floor resume, real rescued worker admission, durable salvage and consumables, persistent NPC wounds, occupied-body exclusivity, warned guard combat/fines, full-ground-loot pre-fatal admission, recall and saturated-bank recovery, actual trespass boundaries, local remembered reactions, honest signs and floor-scoped transient audio. The coordinator’s cold real-world probe is `.dream-loop/living-systems/underworld/coordinator-survey.json`: its first Node tick still incurs roughly 103 ms while generating fourteen cold chunks (70.6 ms generation); warmed ticks were approximately 0.5–2.7 ms. This is an explicit remaining native-integration profiling concern for new-town discovery, not a claimed phone performance success. Initial activation can run during the existing title/transition warm-up; the lead’s native profiler must verify active traversal separately.

## Recall, effect receipts and observer consequences

`{kind:'underworld-recover'}` is an explicit expedition mind recall for an authenticated current underground resident. It is available when the legacy client reports fainting and as a deliberate manual recall. The room does **not** have an independently authoritative legacy player-health model, so this does not claim to prove death. It returns the exact stored entry address, or the first clear cardinal landing within three tiles if that address is obstructed. No clear landing means no debit and no transition. Ordinary home travel remains physical pathfinding.

Recall loses `ceil(20% × authoritative field coins)` (at least one coin when nonzero; zero when destitute), with no grant or healing effect. It returns a canonical `transition.reason:'recall'` and `recovery:{coinLoss,cooldownUntil}`. The resident retains its sequence, loss and thirty-second cooldown across saves, disconnects and re-entry. Owned projectiles and defensive windows are cancelled. The lead session may continue its existing clinic/mind recovery only after this confirmed return; it must not apply a local clinic teleport while the room still considers the life underground. `frame.recall` exposes the real cost/cooldown for the UI.

`FieldEconomy.forfeitCoins` is a narrow loss-only authority method used in the same synchronous transaction as the resident recall mutation. It rejects negative/fractional/unsafe amounts and overdrafts; it never creates a reward receipt or touches items. Therefore a saturated permanent reward ledger cannot trap a traveler who needs recall. Replay protection remains in the coordinator's durable command floor and resident recall sequence. Save/checkpoint atomicity must include the economy and resident state together.

Every coordinator command now projects adapter outcomes onto explicit `SystemsResult` keys. Internal bank rewards, drop bodies, guild member records and transient event arrays are not receipt payloads. A root-stamped `receipt:{scope,sequence,actorId,targetBodyId}` lets the existing boundary apply restoration once to the intended life/body; the separate receipt helper owns validation and client application. The frame remains the source of inventory and property state. `drainEvents()` is ephemeral, with optional `spaceId` defaulting to surface; underground hit/cue, supply and loot events explicitly carry their floor so equal coordinates cannot leak audio between spaces. These queues are never saved. Wards reduce **guard and underground attack damage** by 30% for thirty seconds; they do not promise mitigation or fauna repulsion for unbridged legacy damage sources. `frame.activeWardSeconds` reports the remaining authority duration.

Closed doors now block civic sight. A bounded per-life saved occupancy record detects actual accepted surface entry into a denied building, including one whose physical door another NPC already opened. Repeated interior poses do not re-offend; leaving and re-entering creates a new remembered action. Initial spawn/reconnect does not fabricate an entry, blocked poses do not count, and underground transitions do not become surface trespass. Real estate/guest permissions, verified solo homes and a trusted occupied body's actual original household all authorize entry. Witness knowledge still requires the existing local sight/hearing rules; there is no instant town-wide identification.

`frame.reactions` contains at most 32 visible unoccupied people, with `npcId`, actual remembered `summary`, `hostile`/`fear` booleans and `priceMultiplier`. Each derives from that observer's identified evidence and temperament, not the town aggregate. The root uses these facts for dialogue/trade reactions. Signs now advertise only actual owned storage/furnishings/stations, real workbenches and present merchants/guild contacts; their ownership/access comes from the same authority used for entry. `frame.benefits` exposes only implemented benefits: craft-rank-one `workshopAccess`, eligible rank-two `serviceDiscount:0.1`, and rescued-plan `rebuilding:[{station,coinDiscount:0.25}]`. These do not discount acquisition, deposits, transfers or rents.
