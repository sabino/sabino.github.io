## Design Specificity Verdict

**The opening is strongly authored for Sabino Software; the middle and lower page become category-familiar.**

The system-topology artwork, live pipeline, i3-style terminal, cyan/lime signals, file labels, explicit maturity states, and restricted-case treatment are not generic “AI agency” decoration. They express the actual proposition: a founder-led engineer using automation and agents to stay close to systems that cannot fail. Another consultancy could not reuse this hero unchanged without lying.

Specificity weakens after the hero. Capabilities, case studies, restricted work, projects, journal, founder story, and contact settle into a familiar portfolio sequence built from many similarly weighted dark cards. The content remains unusually credible, but the repeated card grammar makes the page feel progressively more templated.

## Overall Impression

This is already a distinctive, credible technical-consulting site. It looks like someone who understands systems designed it, rather than a marketer applying a startup template. Its biggest weakness is not lack of craft; it is lack of editorial restraint. The homepage tries to prove everything before allowing the visitor to decide.

The result is a strong first five minutes followed by a long evidence marathon. English reaches **13,797px desktop / 21,236px mobile**; Portuguese reaches **14,386px desktop / 21,943px mobile**. That length dilutes rather than compounds the strongest proof.

## What’s Working

1. **The promise becomes physical.** “Systems that have to work” is reinforced by the topology, terminal activity, delivery sequence, and founder-accountability copy. The hero does not merely say “engineering excellence”; it demonstrates a working philosophy.

2. **Trust is handled with unusual discipline.** Career logos disclaim endorsement, confidential work is described through safe engineering surfaces, experiments declare their maturity, and Felipe Sabino remains personally accountable. This makes the page persuasive without turning it into inflated agency copy.

3. **English and Portuguese behave like parallel products.** Both retain equivalent structure, localized interaction states, metadata, contact copy, and intentional hero wrapping. Neither language overflowed horizontally, and switching language preserved the current section.

## Cognitive Load

**Moderate: 3 of 8 checklist failures.**

- **Chunking fails:** the systems lab exposes seven projects plus GitHub, while the career rail creates 17 focusable logo stops.
- **Minimal choices fails:** desktop navigation presents seven destinations/actions; the lab presents eight exits.
- **Progressive disclosure fails:** three full cases, four restricted extracts, seven projects, three journal entries, founder narrative, and four principles are all expanded on one homepage.

Single focus, grouping, visual hierarchy, sequential reading, and working-memory support remain good. The hero’s primary action is clear. The problem is cumulative: the visitor repeatedly faces another complete collection rather than a curated next decision.

## Emotional Journey

The hero is the peak: decisive promise, memorable systems imagery, live operational detail, and personal accountability. The experience disclaimer, delivery rail, and flagship cases then build reassurance.

The emotional valley begins after those cases. Restricted work, the systems lab, and journal produce several screens of similarly weighted evidence before the visitor encounters Felipe Sabino as a person. The founder section restores warmth and trust.

The intended ending peak is private, direct contact. In the locally observed unavailable state, the disabled button and generic “try again soon” copy turn that moment into another valley. LinkedIn is a valid fallback, but it is spatially separate from the failure state.

## Priority Issues

### 1. [P1] Closed mobile navigation exposes invisible keyboard targets

**Why it matters:** With `aria-expanded="false"`, `opacity: 0`, and `pointer-events: none`, keyboard focus still reaches hidden navigation links before the hero. Keyboard and switch users navigate through an interface they cannot see.

**Fix:** Apply `inert` and `aria-hidden` while closed; move focus into the opened menu; restore focus to the menu button on close; contain focus while the modal-style menu is active.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 2. [P2] The homepage acts like the complete evidence archive

**Why it matters:** The page’s extraordinary length makes the visitor decide how much proof is “enough.” Strong cases lose impact because they are followed by several more equally weighted collections, and there is no contextual conversion moment after the main proof.

**Fix:** Keep two flagship public cases, one public-safe extract, and three representative lab artifacts on the homepage. Move the remainder into dedicated case and project indexes. Add a contextual conversation CTA immediately after production proof.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### 3. [P2] Contact failure recovery is passive at the highest-stakes moment

**Why it matters:** A visitor who has finally decided to contact Sabino Software receives a disabled submit button and generic waiting language. The alternative channel is not presented inside the failure state.

**Fix:** Add `Retry secure form` and `Continue on LinkedIn` directly to the status region, preserve entered values, and distinguish temporary initialization from persistent failure. This finding applies to the rendered fallback and does not claim the production endpoint is currently down.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 4. [P2] Functional/support text and secondary targets are too small

**Why it matters:** Form labels around 10px, footer navigation around 10.7px, and mobile links with 17–18px-high boxes undermine legibility and touch accuracy. Meanwhile, 17 focusable company logos add keyboard work without offering actions.

**Fix:** Raise functional/support typography to a readable floor, make link rows at least 44px tall on touch layouts, and remove logos from the tab order unless they expose genuinely interactive content.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### 5. [P2] Global lowercase conflicts with technical precision

**Why it matters:** `body { text-transform: lowercase; }` changes AI, AWS, GCP, Azure, EC2, EKS, and other official technical names. That contradicts the product commitment that editorial lowercase should not corrupt acronyms or proper names.

**Fix:** Apply lowercase only to intentional editorial classes. Explicitly preserve company names, technologies, acronyms, project metadata, and role titles.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time visitor:** The primary CTA is clear, but technical terms such as RAG, CDC, EKS, dbt, and “agentic” appear before a plain explanation of the kinds of problems a client can bring. Seven navigation choices and eight project exits make Jordan unsure how much evidence must be reviewed.

**Riley, stress tester:** A Turnstile/config failure disables the form without a direct retry. Closed mobile navigation accepts invisible focus, and focusable non-action logos imply behavior they do not provide.

**Casey, distracted mobile visitor:** The homepage spans roughly 25 mobile screens. After passing the hero CTA, Casey gets no contextual conversion prompt until the end, while many secondary links are below a comfortable touch-target size.

**Rafael, CTO/founder carrying a critical system:** The cases establish depth, but Rafael must assemble the likely first engagement from distant sections. A compact “problem → first engagement → concrete artifact → handoff” explanation would reduce perceived risk without inventing metrics.

## Minor Observations

- The universal `↗` treatment makes same-page anchors and the submit button look like external navigation.
- The desktop CTA row straddles the 900px fold by approximately 6px; the mobile CTAs fit inside the first viewport.
- The persistent desktop contact CTA is useful, but mobile hides that conversion path inside the menu.
- Focus styling, skip links, semantic landmarks, form labels, live status, and reduced-motion handling provide a solid accessibility foundation.
- The Portuguese mobile hero fits with only about 11px remaining on the right; it is valid now but should remain a responsive regression target.
- The form preserves entered values after a network failure, providing a good base for improved recovery.
